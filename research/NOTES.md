### OPEN NOMINATION BROKE MY HEADLINE CLAIM — the most valuable result here
Tier 2 claimed, at Tier A: the July 2025 EDA-for-rare-earth-magnets exchange
shows DELTA values minerals > semiconductors and OMEGA the reverse, because each
conceded the issue it valued less.

Two independent challenges, both sourced:

1. TIMING. The EDA restrictions were imposed in MAY 2025 and lifted in JULY 2025
   — "less than six weeks after the original restrictions took effect". So the
   sequence is: US escalates -> PRC counter-escalates -> both return to status
   quo ante. Mutual de-escalation of a fresh tit-for-tat is NOT a log-roll. A
   log-roll creates joint value by trading across differently-valued issues;
   undoing reciprocal harm just restores a status quo both preferred, which is
   closer to escaping a prisoner's dilemma than to integrative bargaining.

2. THE MINERALS LEVER IS TEMPORARY BY DESIGN. RFF models it formally as a
   repeated game (citing Fudenberg & Maskin 1986): permanent restriction would
   induce ROW investment in alternatives and destroy China's market position, so
   "China exerts temporary leverage while avoiding long-term damage to its own
   processing industry." If restriction was never meant to be permanent, OMEGA
   conceding it is WEAK evidence about relative valuation.

3. ASYMMETRIC DURABILITY. War on the Rocks (Camba, 5 Jan 2026) scores the two
   choke points on five dimensions — durability, replaceability, precision,
   feedback, sustainability — and concludes "America's semiconductor choke point
   cuts deeper and endures longer than China's rare earth ban." Precision:
   a rare-earth ban "forces China's midstream manufacturers to absorb much of the
   shock they intend to impose on foreign buyers." Feedback: "each use of a choke
   point weakens its impact." Buffers: DLA stockpiles months; automakers ~1yr.
   RFF calls it a "one-shot bazooka".

WHAT SURVIVES:
 - Export controls of both kinds are the active bargaining currency between
   these parties, and are traded against EACH OTHER. Taiwan is not in that trade.
 - The case's shipped log-roll (export controls vs Taiwan arms) is unsupported.
WHAT DOES NOT:
 - The specific orientation claim, at Tier A. DOWNGRADE to Tier B, moderate
   confidence, with the de-escalation reading flagged as a live alternative.
 - "Symmetric opposed-intensity pair" — the arms differ in durability, so a
   static point schedule mis-specifies at least the minerals side.
