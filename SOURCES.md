# Sourced structure: the 12-cell matrix

Tier 0+ per `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §9.4 — direction *and* intensity for 6 issues ×
2 roles, from primary documents, single coder, no reliability statistics.
Evidence hierarchy per §3: **A** revealed concessions › **B** linkage statements
› **C** declared salience › **D** expert assessment › **E** journalism.

DELTA = US executive branch (§2). Congressional preferences enter through the
BATNA, so USCC and Select Committee material is coded as a *distinct actor* and
is not read directly as DELTA intensity.

## Cell notes and locators

### 1–2 · `export_controls` — the highest-intensity issue for *both* sides

**DELTA.** The 2025 NSS contains "really only one operational economic policy…
the building of secure supply chains" ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/breaking-down-trumps-2025-national-security-strategy/)).
On the congressional side, 5 of the 10 priority (▲) recommendations in the USCC
2025 report are technology-restriction items — ▲1 a consolidated economic
statecraft entity for "China's systematic and persistent evasion of U.S. export
controls and sanctions"; ▲10 directing BIS to shift advanced chips "from a
'sell' model to a 'rent' model"; plus ▲8 (classified), ▲9, ▲11
(`research/uscc_exec.txt` L3543, L3814, L3751, L3754, L3870). "Export control"
appears 34 times in the executive summary; "arms sale" appears **zero** times.

**OMEGA.** This is the only issue on which Beijing spends material leverage.
MOFCOM acts under the Export Control Law to bar gallium, germanium, antimony and
superhard materials to the US (Dec 2024), then trades a rare-earth pause at Busan
(Oct 2025). The framing is explicitly that of a calibrated, reversible
instrument:

> 中国的出口管制不是禁止出口，对符合规定的申请将予以许可
> ([MOFCOM via gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202510/content_7044134.htm))

and Beijing counts its list against Washington's — "美方管制清单物项超过3000项，
而中方出口管制清单物项仅900余项" (same source). The 20th Party Congress work
report §5 frames chokepoint technology as a battle to be won: "坚决打赢关键核心
技术攻坚战" ([12371.cn](https://www.12371.cn/2022/10/25/ARTI1666705047474465.shtml)).

**Consequence: the two sides' top-ranked issue is the same one.** That is the
finding that breaks the log-roll — see `PROPOSAL.md` §2.

### 3–4 · `security_commitment` — high declared value, low revealed spend

**OMEGA.** Taiwan is red line #1 of four, and technology restrictions fall under
the *fourth* ([CFR](https://www.cfr.org/articles/unpacking-chinas-four-red-lines-and-its-warning-trump)).
The response to arms sales is materially *thinner*, though not empty — a
distinction the first pass got wrong. MOFA's December 2024 statement lodges
"serious protests at once" and urges the US to "immediately stop arming Taiwan"
without announcing countermeasures
([MOFA](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/xw/fyrbt/202412/t20241222_11514772.html)),
but Beijing separately sanctioned 13 US military firms over that same package
(Reuters, 5 December 2024, cited at `research/uscc_full.txt`). **Correction:** my
first pass claimed Beijing takes no material action on arms sales. It does. The
weight to give it is that sanctioning US defence firms already barred from the
Chinese market is close to costless, where the gallium and germanium bans cost
Chinese exporters real revenue. The revealed-intensity ranking survives; the flat
claim did not.
The 2022 white paper lists arms sales as one grievance among several under
external interference — "不断策动对台军售，加深美台军事勾连" (§三(四)) — rather
than as a standalone demand ([full text](https://bw.china-embassy.gov.cn/sgxw/202208/t20220810_10740353.htm)).
Force is reserved specifically against "外部势力干涉和极少数'台独'分裂分子"
(work report §13). Under §3's hierarchy, revealed spend outranks declared
salience, which puts this **second** to export controls.

**DELTA — contradictory, adjudicated.** Trump on a $14bn package: *"I'm holding
that in abeyance and it depends on China. It's a very good negotiating chip for
us, frankly"* ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trumps-comment-about-negotiations-on-taiwan-heightens-concerns-over-china)).
Against that: Rubio's "there has been no change in US policy toward Taiwan", a
Taiwanese official's "arms sales are negotiated solely with Taipei", and the
report that Trump "did not allow Xi to negotiate or haggle with him over Taiwan
arms sales" ([Taipei Times, 17 May 2026](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/05/17/2003857476)).
The base flow is undisturbed throughout — $11.44bn notified across 2025,
including $11.11bn in December 2025 across eight notifications
([Forum on the Arms Trade](https://www.forumarmstrade.org/ustaiwan.html)) — and
the August 2026 hold has a competing explanation in Iran-war munitions stocks.

> **Adjudication.** The *baseline flow* is not traded; *specific increments* are
> held as leverage, in the President's own words. The issue is tradeable, but the
> tradeable object is the increment, not the relationship.

This retracts the claim in the previous proposal that Washington "has declined
to haggle" — too strong, and contradicted by the principal's own statement.

### 5–6 · `tariffs` — confirmed as the closest thing to a pure transfer

Geneva was point-for-point: 145%→30% against 125%→10%, ~115 points each
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/5/12/china-and-us-agree-90-day-tariff-suspension-as-trade-war-talks-extended)).
Busan moved the aggregate 57%→47%
([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-happened-when-trump-met-xi/)).
Tariff relief is consistently what Beijing *extracts* and what Washington *pays
with*, which is the signature of a distributive issue rather than an end in
itself. USCC mentions "tariff" 9 times and carries no tariff priority
recommendation.

### 7–8 · `precursors` — falsified on Tier A evidence

At Busan the US halved fentanyl tariffs 20%→10% and China agreed to "restore
cooperation on fentanyl precursor flows"
([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-happened-when-trump-met-xi/)).
Jonathan Czin characterises this as a return to "the status quo ante" — a
withdrawn cooperation **restored**, not a new one offered.

> A compatible issue is one both sides want independently and neither has to pay
> for. Cooperation that costs ten percentage points of tariff relief, and that
> had been withdrawn in order to be sold back, is a bargaining chip.

Corroboration on the analytic tier: Brookings assesses Chinese cooperation as
"transactional compliance disconnected from genuine commitment"
([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/us-china-relations-and-fentanyl-and-precursor-cooperation-in-2024/)).
Note the earlier draft rested this finding on that Tier D assessment alone; the
Busan exchange is Tier A and much stronger.

Independently: "fentanyl" and "precursor" appear **zero** times in the USCC 2025
executive summary. Its 11 "pharmaceutical" mentions are ▲19, on US *dependence*
on Chinese APIs and key starting materials — a different issue.

### 9–10 · `market_access` — interior optimum confirmed

**OMEGA** is unilaterally opening on its own terms: the national foreign-investment
negative list is down to 29 items with manufacturing restrictions "全面取消"
([Xinhua](http://www.news.cn/fortune/20240909/603dca3f358146778846d00ad637938c/c.html);
[MOFCOM](https://www.mee.gov.cn/zcwj/zclcfh/202501/t20250108_1100186.shtml)),
and the work report §4 commits to "合理缩减外资准入负面清单，依法保护外商投资权益".
Monotonic preference for openness — confirmed.

**DELTA** wants reciprocity *with* safeguards, not maximal openness: USCC asks
for a multilateral forum "taking into consideration issues of reciprocal market
access and ensuring fair treatment for U.S. exporters in third countries"
(`research/uscc_exec.txt` L4193) while "investment" appears 52 times, largely in
screening and restriction contexts. A non-monotonic interior optimum is the right
shape.

### 11–12 · `joint_research` — compatible with a low ceiling, confirmed

The S&T Agreement was renewed 13 December 2024 for five years but narrowed to
government-to-government cooperation in pre-identified basic-research areas
(earthquake studies, basic health), with sensitive and emerging technologies
explicitly excluded ([State Dept](https://2021-2025.state.gov/amendment-and-extension-of-the-u-s-prc-science-and-technology-agreement-sta/);
[CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/us-china-science-and-technology-cooperation-agreement-not-yet-obsolete)).
Both governments signed. Small positive value to both sides with a hard ceiling
is exactly what `compatible_minor` encodes.

## Bearing on the Quarantine case

`deconfliction_channel` should be **upgraded**, not hedged. The previous draft
called it "only partially compatible" on the strength of Beijing's 2022
suspension. The 2025–26 record is of routinised cooperation that *both* militaries
publicise: MMCA working groups in Hawaii 18–20 November 2025 and 28–29 May 2026,
with the PRC Ministry of National Defence, Xinhua and CGTN all reporting agreement
that the meetings "contribute to avoiding misunderstandings, preventing
miscalculations, and managing risks and crises", conducted "on the basis of
equality and respect" ([PRC MND](http://eng.mod.gov.cn/2025xb/N/T/16423311.html);
[Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20251122/a71ac3114cf345d18cbf52fa883682c2/c.html)).
PRC state media voluntarily publicising military cooperation with the US is a
strong compatibility signal.

Operational detail for the option ladders, from CSIS: a China Coast Guard–led
"law enforcement operation" with MSA support and PLA ISR; "enhanced customs
inspection rules" requiring advance declarations; vessels "authorized to board
vessels, conduct on-site inspections, question personnel"; enforcement through
"fines and market access restrictions". Scenario 1 — one main port, 48-hour
window, ~one week. Scenario 2 — "significantly scaled up", "over two weeks"
([CSIS ChinaPower](https://features.csis.org/chinapower/china-quarantine-taiwan/)).
Only 13% of surveyed US experts and 9% of Taiwan experts were "completely
confident" the US would intervene militarily, which supports the direction of the
case's BATNA asymmetry (OMEGA 36 > DELTA 34).

## Second pass: what reading the full texts changed

The first pass reached every source through `WebFetch`, which returns a small
model's *summary* rather than the document. Six primaries were then pulled down
and read locally. Spot-checking the pass-1 quotations against source found them
accurate but lossy — the work-report passage on force is truncated in the
summary, and ends "…及其分裂活动，**绝非针对广大台湾同胞**" (absolutely not
directed at the broad mass of Taiwan compatriots). 以台制华 occurs six times in
the white paper; the summary surfaced one.

**Frequencies shift materially between the USCC executive summary and the full
745-page report.** Full report: tariff 249 · semiconductor 241 · export control
223 · rare earth 136 · blockade 21 · market access 18 · arms sale 11 · precursor
10 · quarantine 3 · fentanyl 2. The executive summary put "tariff" at 9. Cells 5
and 6 should therefore be read at **higher** intensity than the first pass coded
them; the export-controls-over-arms-sales ratio (223:11) is unchanged in
direction.

**`precursors` gains a third, independent source.** The one substantive fentanyl
mention in 745 pages records tariffs being *imposed* over "lack of cooperation on
cracking down on the shipment of fentanyl precursors to North America" — the same
lever Busan later ran in reverse. Imposed for non-cooperation, relaxed for
cooperation. That is a price, not a shared preference.

**The white paper's grievance list is ordered.** Arms sales appear as item 3 of 7
under 三(四), inside a list led by hollowing out one-China and official
exchanges. The organising frame is 以台制华 — and Taiwan is explicitly a 棋子, a
chess piece, used "把台湾当作遏制中国发展进步…的棋子". In Beijing's own account,
Taiwan pressure is instrumental to blocking China's *development*, which is
where export controls sit.

**MOFCOM detail the summary omitted.** China notified other governments in
advance "通过双边出口管制对话机制" — a standing bilateral export-control dialogue
channel. It signals a licensing ladder rather than a binary: "积极考虑适用通用
许可、许可豁免等多种便利化措施". It grieves the US de minimis rule "低至0%". And
it records offering industry cooperation on shipbuilding, which the US declined —
a *rejected integrative offer*, directly relevant to what the eval measures.

Carried through two drafts: *"CFR shows technology restrictions rank fourth — the

### The finding that changes the recommendation

The case pairs US export controls against Taiwan arms sales as its log-roll. The
one documented instance of these two parties actually exchanging concessions
pairs export controls against **each other**:

> "In July 2025, BIS rescinded license requirements for EDA firms **after the PRC
> agreed to resume licensing rare earth magnets for U.S. firms**."
> — CRS R48642, Tier A

With two corroborating instruments in the same report: "BIS reportedly withheld
export control actions against China during U.S.-China tariff talks", and the
August 2025 approval of Nvidia's H20 and AMD's MI308 "under terms that the U.S.
government would receive 15% of proceeds".

**Semiconductors against critical minerals is a genuine opposed-intensity pair.**
Each side is intense on the control it wants lifted and less intense on the one
it grants — which is exactly the structure `log_roll_delta_concedes` /
`log_roll_omega_concedes` encodes. This is the constructive fix: the log-roll is
not missing from US–China relations, it is *mis-seated* in the case.

Documented rungs for the option ladders, from the same report — US: Entity List
(actor-based) → foreign direct product rule → performance thresholds (total
processing performance, performance density) → country-wide controls → AI
Diffusion tiers I/II/III → rescission → case-by-case licence with revenue share.
PRC: gallium, germanium and rare-earth magnet controls; antidumping; SAMR
antitrust; and 2020 antitrust terms compelling Nvidia to let PRC buyers purchase
a year's supply.

A congressional constraint on DELTA, supporting the plan's §2 decision to route
Congress through the BATNA: former officials object to "negotiating national
security decisions in exchange for trade concessions or government revenue…
contradicting past U.S. practice".

### Second exclusion finding — the Section 301 report

215 pages, and the legal foundation of the entire tariff war. Frequencies:
technology transfer **227** · cyber **247** · licensing **113** · joint venture
**78** · tariff 10 · export control 2 · **Taiwan 1**.

Like Phase One, it excludes Taiwan and export controls almost entirely and rests
on forced technology transfer, JV and licensing requirements, and cyber-enabled
IP theft. Two foundational US instruments, independently, define the negotiable
set as trade, IP, technology transfer and market access — with Taiwan in a
separate channel.

### The Anti-Secession Law settles the Taiwan cell

> 第八条 "台独"分裂势力以任何名义、任何方式造成台湾从中国分裂出去的事实，或者
> 发生将会导致台湾从中国分裂出去的重大事变，或者和平统一的可能性完全丧失，国家
> 得采取非和平方式及其他必要措施

Three statutory triggers: separation in fact; a major incident leading to it; the
complete loss of the possibility of peaceful unification. **Arms sales are not a
trigger.** Binding law, not rhetoric, places them below the threshold — which is
consistent with rank 2 and inconsistent with treating the issue as a red line.

### Quarantine — CSIS wargame detail

26 iterations: 19 with escalation fixed, 5 free-play. Two primary variables
(China's escalation level against the US response). The base rung of the ladder
is "board ships bound to Taiwan using the Chinese Coast Guard and the People's
Armed Forces Maritime Militia". Findings: "blockades almost always have
casualties. They are not bloodless military operations"; "there are escalatory
pressures at every level, sometimes on one side, sometimes on the other"; "Taiwan
cannot face China alone."

Most relevant to this eval, the free-play games produced divergent outcomes from
identical setups — "In some games, the teams went to a high level of violence. In
other games, they found an offramp" — which is the same quantity this eval
measures, in a human-subject design. And CSIS locates the quarantine variant
squarely where this case puts it: "The quarantine element falls more in the
policy domain."

### The log-roll is now confirmed with its orientation, by a completed trade

Tier 0+ established that the log-roll is mis-seated. Tier 2 establishes which
way the corrected pair points, and it does so from an exchange that actually
happened:

> "In July 2025, BIS rescinded license requirements for EDA firms after the PRC
> agreed to resume licensing rare earth magnets for U.S. firms." — CRS R48642

| | conceded | won | therefore values |
|---|---|---|---|
| DELTA / US | EDA — **semiconductors** | rare-earth magnets | **minerals > semiconductors** |
| OMEGA / PRC | magnets — **minerals** | EDA relief | **semiconductors > minerals** |

Each side gave up its lower-valued issue to win its higher-valued one. That is
opposed intensity — **membership and orientation both at Tier A**, which is a
stronger evidentiary basis than any cell in the shipped design.

Magnitude, from the analytic layer: "China accounted for over 90 percent of
global production of neodymium (rare earth) magnets in 2024" (IEA via
[PIIE](https://www.piie.com/blogs/realtime-economics/2026/trump-china-trade-wars-five-takeaways-us-imports-2025)),
against a 2024 US government survey in which 44% of companies did not know
whether their chips came from China. CSIS frames the PRC action explicitly as
retaliation, in its own question header: "How has China retaliated against the
United States for this week's restrictions?"

**One caveat, from the NSS.** DELTA's minerals interest is framed as supply
*access*, which diversification can also satisfy — coalitions, Africa, allied
production. OMEGA's minerals leverage therefore has a substitute and should
decay over time in a way its semiconductor grievance does not. Worth modelling
as a note on the issue, not a reason to drop it.

### Tariffs: a material fact the earlier passes missed entirely

> "The US Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against many of Trump's 2025
> tariffs forced him to recalibrate his entire trade strategy." — PIIE

New Section 301 investigations followed in March 2026. Current levels as of
August 2026: US average **47.5%** on Chinese goods, China average **31.9%** on
US goods, both at 100% coverage. The case models tariffs as a symmetric pure
transfer (20 points each side); the record shows a standing asymmetry of roughly
16 percentage points and a legal ceiling the earlier passes did not know about.

### Quarantine: RAND triangulates CSIS and revises the walk-away values

RAND RRA1279-1, *Implications of a Coercive Quarantine of Taiwan by the PRC*,
38 pages. Its findings are section headings:

> "The Economic Balance of Power Between Taiwan and China Favors the PRC" —
> "the PRC has effectively every advantage over Taiwan" · "Direct Pressure Will
> Likely Be Necessary to Break the Quarantine" · "A Counterblockade Is Unlikely
> to Be Successful" · "The Force Requirements for the United States Are Likely
> to Be Heavy"

And on the character of the action: "Quarantine is not a low-risk, slow-moving
action, equivalent to imposing economic sanctions. Regardless of how it is
imposed, such an action is likely to rapidly escalate to use of force."

**The BATNA asymmetry in `quarantine.yaml` (DELTA 34, OMEGA 36) is directionally
right and too narrow.** RAND adds that it worsens dynamically: "the PRC's very
success in establishing asymmetry may in fact reduce the choices available to
the United States", and "once a quarantine is imposed, ambiguity is no longer a
possibility… inaction is tantamount to accepting the PRC's actions."

**Adjudication.** CSIS holds that a blockade "wouldn't be a low-risk, low-cost
option for Beijing"; RAND holds that the PRC has "effectively every advantage".
These are reconcilable once the comparison class is separated — PRC-against-Taiwan
is dominant, PRC-against-the-world is contested — and RAND says so itself: the
PRC "is not in an impregnable position relative to the rest of the world."

Most usefully, RAND locates the negotiable space precisely:

> "While there may be room to negotiate the movement of **particular kinds of
> commodities**, if the PRC declines to allow free shipment, no amount of
> indirect pressure is likely to result in the PRC abandoning its efforts."

The tradeable object in a quarantine is *which cargoes move*, not whether the
quarantine ends — which is a sourced specification for the `quarantine_scope`
ladder, currently authored.

### A third party the case does not model

RAND's gray-zone tabletop exercise (CFA2065-1, 45 pages) found Blue (US) and
Green (Taiwan) valuing the same events differently: "Both viewed existential
threat… as their redline for military escalation, but Green rated sovereignty
challenges as more threatening than Blue", and "Green was often disappointed by
the lack of specificity in Blue's promises of support." Both cases are strictly
bilateral. The record documents a consequential third party whose preferences
diverge from DELTA's — a limitation, now stated.

## Tier 3: open nomination, concordance, published corpus

**Tier 3's defining requirement is a second independent coder** with
Krippendorff's α (§6, §9). There is one coder here. A second reading by me is
correlated with the first in exactly the way α exists to detect, so any α
reported would be a fabricated number. **Not attempted.** Three things that *are*
legitimately available to a single coder were done instead.

### 1 · Open nomination, run adversarially — and it broke the headline claim

Rather than nominating sources likely to extend the findings, the search targeted
what would destroy them. It succeeded against the strongest one.

Tier 2 read the July 2025 EDA-for-magnets exchange as a log-roll and inferred its
orientation at Tier A. Two independent challenges:

**Timing.** The EDA restrictions were imposed in **May 2025** and lifted in
**July 2025** — under six weeks. As a sequence that is escalation,
counter-escalation, return to status quo ante. Mutual de-escalation is not a
log-roll: a log-roll creates joint value by trading across differently-valued
issues, while undoing reciprocal harm restores a position both sides already
preferred. That is escaping a prisoner's dilemma, not integrative bargaining.

**The lever is temporary by design.** Resources for the Future models rare-earth
restriction as a repeated game, citing Fudenberg and Maskin (1986): permanent
restriction would induce rest-of-world investment in alternatives and destroy
China's market position, so "China exerts temporary leverage while avoiding
long-term damage to its own processing industry." If the restriction was never
meant to be permanent, relinquishing it says little about relative valuation.

**And the arms are not symmetric.** War on the Rocks scores both choke points on
durability, replaceability, precision, feedback and sustainability, concluding
that "America's semiconductor choke point cuts deeper and endures longer than
China's rare earth ban". A rare-earth ban "forces China's midstream manufacturers
to absorb much of the shock they intend to impose on foreign buyers", and "each
use of a choke point weakens its impact." RFF calls it a "one-shot bazooka"; DLA
stockpiles run months and automakers hold about a year.

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| Export controls of both kinds are the live currency, traded against each other, Taiwan outside | **survives** |
| The shipped log-roll (export controls vs Taiwan arms) is unsupported | **survives** |
| Orientation: minerals > semiconductors for DELTA, reverse for OMEGA | **Tier A → Tier B, moderate** |
| A symmetric opposed-intensity pair | **withdrawn** |

## What this is not

Single coder, so **no reliability statistics** — per-cell confidence is reported
instead. Cardinal magnitudes and BATNA levels remain synthetic by design and were
never in scope (§1). The ~14 medium analytic sources that would provide
triangulation are the Tier 2 layer and were not read; cells 3–4 and 9–10 carry
moderate rather than high confidence largely for that reason. Chinese-language
sources were read in the original; no independent translation check was performed.
